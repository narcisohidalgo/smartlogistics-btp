# SmartLogistics Weather Risk & Route Response

## Purpose

This procedure defines how logistics operators should respond when weather conditions may affect a shipment route or planned delivery.

## Scope

This procedure applies when the SmartLogistics weather integration reports a MEDIUM or HIGH weather risk, or when a carrier reports weather-related disruption.

## Weather Risk Levels

### LOW

Weather conditions are not expected to significantly affect transport operations.

Recommended response:
- Continue normal shipment monitoring.
- No additional escalation is required.
- Refresh the weather forecast if the planned arrival date changes significantly.

### MEDIUM

Weather conditions may affect route conditions, travel time, or delivery reliability.

Recommended response:
- Review the latest weather forecast.
- Contact the carrier if the route may be affected.
- Confirm whether additional travel time is expected.
- Monitor weather conditions more frequently.
- Review active shipment incidents for combined operational risk.

### HIGH

Weather conditions create a significant risk to transport safety, route availability, or delivery reliability.

Recommended response:
- Contact the carrier immediately.
- Confirm whether the current route remains safe and operational.
- Evaluate alternative routes.
- Review the estimated arrival time.
- Notify the destination if a delay is likely.
- Escalate to the logistics supervisor when a major delivery commitment is at risk.

## Combined Risk

Weather conditions should not be evaluated in isolation.

A shipment should be treated as increased operational risk when:

- weather risk is HIGH and there is an active incident;
- weather risk is HIGH and shipment status is DELAYED;
- weather risk is MEDIUM and a HIGH or CRITICAL incident is active;
- multiple route disruptions affect the same shipment.

## Route Decision Guidance

The operator should consider rerouting when:

- the carrier confirms that the planned route is blocked or unsafe;
- severe weather is expected along the planned route;
- the estimated delay on the current route is operationally unacceptable;
- an alternative route provides a safer or more reliable delivery option.

Rerouting should not be recommended solely because weather risk is MEDIUM if the carrier confirms that the route remains safe and operational.

## Carrier Communication

When weather conditions may affect a shipment, request:

- Current shipment location
- Current route
- Route accessibility
- Estimated weather-related delay
- Alternative route options
- Updated estimated arrival time
- Any safety restrictions affecting transport

## Destination Communication

Notify the destination when:

- a weather-related delay is likely to affect the delivery window;
- the shipment has been rerouted;
- the estimated arrival time changes materially;
- unloading or receiving operations may be affected by severe weather.

## Incident Escalation

Create or escalate an incident when weather conditions produce a real operational impact.

Examples include:

- road closure;
- airport or port disruption;
- carrier suspension;
- unsafe route conditions;
- significant weather-related delivery delay.

Weather risk alone does not automatically require creating an incident if no operational disruption has occurred.

## Resolution Criteria

A weather-related incident may be resolved when:

- the route is confirmed operational;
- the shipment has been successfully rerouted;
- the weather disruption has ended;
- the updated delivery plan is confirmed;
- no further operational intervention is required.

## Operator Checklist

Before closing a weather-related issue, confirm:

- Latest weather forecast reviewed
- Current route confirmed
- Carrier contacted when required
- Alternative route evaluated when necessary
- Updated arrival time confirmed
- Destination notified when required
- Related incidents reviewed
- Final mitigation action documented
