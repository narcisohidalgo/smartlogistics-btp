# SmartLogistics Shipment Delay & Escalation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how logistics operators should assess, monitor, communicate, and escalate shipment delays.

## Scope

This policy applies when a shipment is delayed, when the carrier reports a late arrival, or when an incident indicates that planned delivery times may not be met.

## Delay Classification

### LOW

Expected delay of less than 1 hour with no known downstream operational impact.

Recommended response:
- Continue monitoring the shipment.
- Record the delay if operationally relevant.
- No escalation is required unless conditions worsen.

### MEDIUM

Expected delay between 1 and 4 hours.

Recommended response:
- Contact the carrier to confirm the estimated arrival time.
- Review whether the delay affects receiving operations.
- Inform the destination if the delivery window may be missed.
- Keep any related incident open until the revised schedule is confirmed.

### HIGH

Expected delay greater than 4 hours or a delay that affects an important delivery commitment.

Recommended response:
- Escalate to the logistics supervisor.
- Obtain an updated estimated time of arrival from the carrier.
- Evaluate alternative routing or transport options.
- Notify the receiving location.
- Assess whether other shipments or operations depend on the delayed delivery.

### CRITICAL

The shipment cannot continue normally, the delivery cannot be completed within an acceptable timeframe, or the delay causes a major operational or customer impact.

Recommended response:
- Escalate immediately to the logistics supervisor.
- Inform affected operational stakeholders.
- Evaluate alternative carrier, route, or delivery arrangements.
- Record the operational impact and mitigation decision.
- Maintain active monitoring until a recovery plan is confirmed.

## Four-Hour Escalation Rule

A shipment delay should normally be escalated to HIGH when the estimated delay exceeds 4 hours.

A delay below 4 hours may also be escalated to HIGH when:

- the shipment contains time-sensitive goods;
- the delivery window is strict;
- the destination cannot accept a late delivery;
- the delay creates significant downstream operational impact;
- multiple related incidents are active.

## Carrier Communication

When contacting the carrier, the operator should request:

- Current shipment location
- Cause of the delay
- Updated estimated time of arrival
- Current route
- Alternative routing options
- Expected duration of the disruption

## Weather-Related Delays

When weather risk is MEDIUM or HIGH:

- Review the most recent weather information.
- Determine whether the route is affected.
- Ask the carrier whether rerouting is necessary.
- Increase monitoring frequency if conditions may deteriorate.

A HIGH weather risk combined with an active delay should be treated as an increased operational risk.

## Incident Management

A delay incident should remain OPEN or IN_PROGRESS while:

- the final estimated arrival time is uncertain;
- disruption is still active;
- mitigation actions are still being evaluated.

The incident may be RESOLVED when:

- the shipment has recovered its route or delivery plan;
- the updated delivery time is confirmed;
- required notifications have been completed;
- no further operational intervention is required.

## Operator Checklist

Before resolving a shipment delay, confirm:

- Current shipment location
- Cause of delay
- Updated estimated arrival time
- Carrier confirmation
- Destination notification
- Weather impact reviewed
- Required escalation completed
- Mitigation action documented
